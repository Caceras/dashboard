A contract isn't paperwork — it's a tool for surviving the bad month. Every line below has saved a working relationship from ending in a lawyer's email.

## The non-negotiables

- **Scope, in plain English.** Three bullets max. If you can't write it in three bullets, you don't understand it.
- **What's out of scope.** Equally important. List it.
- **Deliverables**, with format. "A 12-page PDF report" not "a report".
- **Timeline**, with the assumptions it depends on. "4 weeks from receipt of brand assets" beats "4 weeks".
- **Payment terms.** 50% upfront, 50% on delivery is the default. Net-30 only for retainers.
- **Late fee.** 1.5% per month after net-30. Just having it in writing pays for itself.
- **Kill fee.** 50% of remaining work if cancelled mid-project. Non-negotiable for anything &gt; 4 weeks.
- **Revisions.** Two rounds included. Third round billed at hourly rate.

## The smart additions

- **Asset hand-off date.** When you need their stuff. If they're late, your timeline shifts.
- **Approval window.** "Silence beyond 5 business days = approval." Stops projects rotting in inbox.
- **Termination clause.** Either party can exit with 30 days' notice. Protects everyone.
- **IP transfer.** On final payment, not before. Until then, the work is yours.

## The clauses to push back on

When a client redlines, expect to see:

- "Unlimited revisions" — replace with a fixed number.
- "Final payment on client satisfaction" — replace with "on delivery", with revisions defined.
- "Work for hire" without scope — only with hourly limits.
- "Non-compete" beyond the engagement — refuse.

## Where it lives in Works

Store the signed PDF link in the client's `notes` field. The contract date and value go on the first project's `started_at` and `budget`. That way every dollar in `/expenses` traces back to a specific contract.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-shield-check" color="success"}
**Tip:** Name your contract files `[client-slug]-[yyyy-mm].pdf` so they sort chronologically forever.
::
